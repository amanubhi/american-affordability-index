# Recommended project scope — pending approval

**Working title:** The American Cost-of-Living Squeeze: Selected U.S. Grocery and Gasoline Prices, January 2015–July 2026.

**Decision question:** How would changes in national average prices alter the modeled cost of a fixed, explicitly illustrative basket of selected groceries and regular gasoline?

Keep 2015 as the reference year where full baseline coverage exists. Label 2026 partial year. Use calendar-window means with observed month counts; do not compare full 2015 to partial 2026 as equivalent annual periods. Define a common comparison window and test same-month / trailing-window sensitivity later.

| Question | Supported scope | Conditions / exclusions |
|---|---|---|
| Q1: increase since 2015 | Item-specific nominal price changes and indexed series | Require verified baseline and comparable endpoint windows; no official inflation claim. |
| Q2: largest changes | Eligible item rankings; reviewed category summaries of item changes | No averaging mixed dollar units; exclude late starts from 2015 rankings. |
| Q3: dollar contributions | Fixed-quantity illustrative basket decomposition | Document quantities and units, choose nonoverlapping items, enforce complete basket rules. Not average household spending. |
| Q4: gasoline/grocery relationship | Regular gasoline versus fixed basket or explicitly weighted grocery change series | Consecutive changes, sample sizes, Pearson/Spearman, lags, rolling/period sensitivity. No causality. |
| Q5: shocks | Observed consecutive-month changes and volatility | Review flags against source; missing months cannot be treated as one-month changes. |
| Q6: future budgets | Three transparent 12-month scenarios | Derive assumptions after historical analysis; scenarios not forecasts. |

Core: selected food items plus regular unleaded gasoline. Exclude utilities, heating oil, diesel and alcohol from core basket; retain in raw data. Choose items after coverage/unit/category review. Do not promise a balanced 139-month basket without resolving gaps.

Defer geographic comparisons, income burden, affordability by household type, consumer substitution, business margins, causal pass-through, and full-year 2026 claims. They require additional data or identification strategies.

No new consumption dataset is necessary if quantities remain illustrative. BLS CPI is necessary only if real-price analysis is included. Establish the nominal-price core first. Power BI, forecasting models and final case-study claims remain outside Milestone 1.
