Read the index: Above 100 means modeled purchasing power improved relative to 2015. The comparison uses aggregate renter income and an illustrative budget, rather than following the same households. Standard ACS income data are missing for 2020.

The budget behind the headline

The 2015 model starts with $1,000 monthly rent, $400 groceries and $150 gasoline. Official price relatives hold this budget structure fixed. Utilities, taxes, healthcare, childcare and other expenses sit outside the model.

These amounts are illustrative assumptions. They are not measured average household expenditure.

Does the result depend on the weights?

Alternative fixed budget weights measure sensitivity. They are not confidence intervals.

Housing pressure across the states

Comparable state rent and income estimates, with DC reported separately. National grocery and gasoline prices do not enter these rankings.

Two distinct measures: The rent/income proxy divides two aggregate medians. Observed burden measures the share of cash-rent households in ACS burden bands of 30% or more, excluding cases where burden could not be computed. Close point-estimate ranks may be statistically indistinguishable.

What changed the budget?

Dollar contributions depend on baseline budget weights. Item rankings and price correlations answer separate questions.

Category changes are unweighted medians of eligible item changes, using complete 2015 and 2024 calendar years. They are not official category inflation or spending contributions. Coffee and potato chips use reviewed labels. Some meat series overlap.
